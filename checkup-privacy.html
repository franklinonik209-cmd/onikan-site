Check Up! privacy policy

Metropolitan Museum of Art, Open Access. License: CC0.

Check Up! is made by Onikan Global Solutions LLC. Contact: contact@onikanglobal.com. This policy describes what the Check Up! app does with your information.

Effective November 1, 2026.

Short version

Check Up! collects no personal data, shares no personal data, and has no account, no server, and no network connection of its own. Everything you do in the game stays on your device, in one encrypted local store. The only things that ever leave your device are the files you yourself choose to share or export, through your own system share sheet or file picker. There are no ads, no analytics, and no crash reporting in the app.

What we do not collect

We do not collect, and the app does not transmit, any of the following:

  • Your name, email address, phone number, or any contact details. There is no sign-in, so there is no account to attach them to.
  • Your location, contacts, photos, microphone recordings, or precise device identifiers. The app asks for none of these.
  • Your advertising ID. There is no advertising SDK in the app, so there is nothing that reads it.
  • Payment card numbers or any other payment data. No purchase is taken today (see section 6), and when Pro subscriptions are activated, payment is handled by Google Play, not by this app.
  • Anything about how you play beyond what section 3 stores locally on your device. No play data is sent anywhere for analytics, advertising, or any other purpose.

What stays on your device

The following live only in the app's local store on your device. Nothing in this list is uploaded, synced, or shared by the app itself:

  • Your profile: a single display name you type yourself. It starts as "Player 1" and you may change it. It is a local label for the table, not an identity.
  • Your settings, your statistics, and your match records.
  • Your cosmetics: the card backs, board skins, table themes, and voice packs you have equipped.
  • Fairness records: the shuffle verification records the game keeps so you can check a shuffle was fair.
  • In pass-and-play games, the display names of the other players at your table. Those names are typed on your device, stored on your device, and never sent anywhere. Hiding one player's hand from the next player is handled on the device by the pass-to interstitial, not by any server.

Storage: the local store is encrypted at rest with AES-256-GCM. The envelope key lives in the Android Keystore or the iOS Keychain on your device. The key is a device-local secret and is never transmitted. Android backup and device transfer are disabled for the app, so the encrypted store and its keys are never separated by a backup. That backup exclusion is scripted in the build workflow; it is re-proved on the release artifact before publication (see Owed).

Audio: all sound in the game (callouts, music bed, effects) is bundled with the app and played from those bundled files. Nothing is streamed, and no audio leaves the device.

What leaves your device, and only when you choose it

Only three kinds of file ever leave the device, and each leaves only because you tapped to send or export it:

  • Brag cards: the win card rendered on your device after a match, sent through your own system share sheet to wherever you choose.
  • Fairness records: the same records described in section 3, shared through your own share sheet when you choose to share one.
  • Backup exports: a file you export yourself, and a backup file you import yourself through the system file picker.

The app itself transmits nothing. It holds no social accounts, embeds no social SDK, and posts nothing on its own. Names on a brag card are the local display names typed at the table; nothing on a card identifies a device, an account, or a person beyond the name the player chose. A backup export file is your file once you export it: if you send it somewhere, that destination's rules apply to it, not this policy.

No advertising, no analytics, no tracking

Check Up! contains no advertising SDK, no analytics SDK, and no crash-reporting SDK. There are no ads in any tier, free or Pro. Nothing in the app tracks you across apps or websites, and nothing is used for advertising. This is a product decision, recorded in the product specification: adding an advertising SDK in the future would require a new specification decision and a rewritten privacy posture, because an ad SDK collects device data by design.

Purchases

No purchase is taken today. The Pro subscription products of record (checkup_pro_monthly and checkup_pro_yearly) and the cosmetic products exist in code only, behind a dark seam, and no price appears in the app. No store products exist yet, and the store is never called while the seam is dark.

When Pro subscriptions are activated, purchases will be handled by Google Play. The app will receive an entitlement (which plan you hold) and will never receive your payment data. At activation, that path is re-checked before any purchase proof is claimed.

Children

Because the app collects nothing and transmits nothing, it collects no personal data from anyone, including children. There is no account, no chat, no messaging, and no user content sent anywhere. A child playing on a device shares nothing with us by playing, and a parent deleting the app deletes everything the game stored (see section 8).

Deleting your data

There is no server copy to delete, because there is no server. Deleting the app deletes the local store with it, including your display name, settings, statistics, match records, cosmetics, and fairness records. A backup export file you saved yourself is not deleted by deleting the app; delete that file yourself if you no longer want it.

If you have a question about this policy, contact contact@onikanglobal.com. There is no account to delete and no developer-held data to return, so questions are answered on that basis.

Changes to this policy

If this policy changes in a material way, the new version will be posted at the same public address before it takes effect, with a new effective date. A change that starts collecting or sharing data would be a new product decision, not a quiet edit: it would require a rewritten data safety record as well as this policy.
