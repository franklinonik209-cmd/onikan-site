KeyPanel privacy policy

Metropolitan Museum of Art, Open Access. License: CC0.

KeyPanel: API Remote is developed and published by Onikan Global Solutions LLC. This policy explains what KeyPanel does with information in the app.

Effective November 1, 2026.

The short version

KeyPanel has no account system, no KeyPanel server, no analytics, and no advertising. We do not collect, receive, sell, or share your API keys, your connection settings, your requests, or your run history. The app stores your configuration on your device and sends requests only to the endpoints you configure, when you run them or schedule them.

Information stored on your device

When you use KeyPanel, the app stores the following on your device so the panel can work:

  • Connections you create: names, base addresses, authentication type, request settings, and the keys or tokens you choose to store
  • Controls, groups, and chains you build, including methods, addresses, headers, and values you enter
  • Run history, capped at the most recent 200 runs
  • App settings, including app lock and auto-lock preferences

Your stored keys and tokens sit in a dedicated vault on the device, encrypted with AES-256-GCM. The vault's master key is held in the platform secure storage provided by your device's operating system. KeyPanel's other stored data is also encrypted on the device under its own key in secure storage. The vault is excluded from automatic operating system cloud backups. No plaintext export of your data exists; the only export is the passphrase-encrypted backup described below.

Keys are write-only inside the app. You can replace a stored key, but KeyPanel never displays it back to you. Only the last four characters are shown, so you can tell keys apart.

Information we collect

We collect nothing. KeyPanel does not ask you to create an account, does not include analytics or advertising software, and does not transmit your keys, configuration, requests, or history to us or to any service we operate. There is no KeyPanel server for the app to send them to.

Where the app sends network requests

KeyPanel is a tool that calls services on your behalf. Its network traffic goes only to destinations involved in that job:

  • The endpoints you configure. When you tap a control, run a test request, or a schedule fires, the app sends the request you built, including the key attached to that connection, directly from your device to that service. That service's own privacy policy governs what it does with requests it receives. A key is sent only to the endpoint on its own connection. If a service redirects a request to a different host, KeyPanel strips the credential headers before following the redirect.
  • The bundled demo connection. KeyPanel includes one demo connection to a public test endpoint (httpbin.org) so you can see a working control immediately. It carries no key and no personal information, sends only a simple test request, and runs only when you tap it. You can delete the demo connection at any time.
  • Sign-in providers you configure. If you set up a connection that uses OAuth2, the sign-in happens in your device's secure browser with the provider you configured, and the resulting tokens are stored in the on-device vault. We never see them.
  • Plain http warning. If you configure a connection on a plain http address with a key attached, the app warns you before saving that the key will travel unencrypted, because anyone on the network path could read it. The choice remains yours, for example for a home lab on your own network.

Imports

You can import cURL commands and Postman collections to draft controls. Importing happens entirely on your device. During import, KeyPanel strips secret material it finds in the source, including credential headers, keys hidden inside web addresses, and address user info, and shows you on a review screen what was removed. Imported drafts never run or schedule themselves.

Backups you export

If you choose to export a backup, KeyPanel creates one file encrypted with a passphrase you set, using PBKDF2 key derivation and AES-256-GCM encryption. The file leaves the app only through the system share sheet, to a destination you pick. We never receive it. Where you store that file, and who can open it with your passphrase, is under your control. Restoring a backup validates the file completely before it replaces current data, and refuses a damaged file rather than overwriting good data.

Purchases

KeyPanel offers an optional Pro upgrade (monthly, annual, or lifetime) with a 7-day free trial on the subscription plans. Purchases are processed by Google Play. We do not receive your payment card details. The app checks your purchase status on your device to unlock Pro features; your configuration and keys are never part of that process. Billing processor, verified from the code on 2026-10-20: no third-party billing provider or billing SDK is integrated anywhere in the app (checked in app/pubspec.yaml and lib/services/billing_service.dart); purchases are handled by Google Play directly. If a billing provider is ever added, this policy will be updated before that version is published.

Sharing

We do not sell personal information, because we do not collect any. We do not share your keys, configuration, or history with advertisers, data brokers, or analytics providers. The only transmissions the app makes are the requests described above, to destinations you configured, plus Google Play's purchase processing if you choose to buy Pro.

Security

Your keys are protected by AES-256-GCM encryption at rest, a master key in your device's platform secure storage, and an optional biometric app lock with configurable auto-lock. Run history and the response inspector pass through a redaction layer that masks authorization headers and token-like values before anything is stored or displayed. If the app lock is on, scheduled runs pause while the vault is locked instead of firing unattended.

No app can promise perfect security, and we do not. An unlocked device in someone else's hands, or a rooted or jailbroken device, weakens every protection described here. Keep your device locked and its software up to date, and use the app lock if your keys matter.

Retention and deletion

Your configuration and history stay in the app's local storage until you change them, delete them inside the app, restore a backup over them, or uninstall the app. Run history keeps at most the 200 most recent runs; older entries are pruned automatically. Uninstalling the app removes its local storage from the device. Backup files you exported earlier remain wherever you saved them, under your control, and you can delete them there.

In-app deletion controls, verified from the code on 2026-10-20: a connection is deleted from its editor (Connections, open the connection, Delete connection), which also removes that connection's stored keys from the vault; a control is deleted from its card menu on the panel (Delete control, after a confirmation); a chain is deleted from the Chains tab (Delete chain, after a confirmation); run history is cleared from the History tab (Clear run history, after a confirmation); restoring a backup replaces the panel and clears the previous run history; uninstalling the app removes all of its local storage.

Changes to this policy
