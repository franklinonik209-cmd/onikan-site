The Finished Audiobook Distribution Checklist

A free checklist from Ghost Narrator, Onikan Global Solutions LLC. Facts checked November 12, 2026. Store rules change, so check each store's current page before you upload.

Use it when your audiobook is finished and mastered. Work top to bottom, tick each box, and stop at the first route that fits your book. You do not need every route. One live listing that sells beats five half-finished uploads.

1. Before you upload: files, format, and the spec

Tick every box here before you open any store portal. A file that fails spec gets rejected everywhere, and each rejection costs you days.

  • Every chapter is its own file, no file longer than 120 minutes.
  • Every file is MP3, 192 kbps or higher, constant bit rate, at 44.1 kHz.
  • Every file measures between -23 dB and -18 dB RMS.
  • Every file peaks no higher than -3 dB.
  • Every file holds a noise floor no higher than -60 dB RMS.
  • 1 to 5 seconds of room tone at the start and end of each file.
  • All files are mono, or all files are stereo. Never a mix.
  • Opening credits and closing credits are in place, as separate files where the store asks for them.
  • Retail sample is 5 minutes or less.
  • Cover art, title, author name, narrator credit, and description are final.
  • You know whether your narration is human or AI. This decides which routes below are open to you: ACX takes human narration unless authorized, and does not accept unauthorized text-to-speech, AI, or automated recordings.

Why these numbers: they are the ACX audio submission requirements, the strictest spec among the major stores. A file that passes ACX passes the others. Check it free first with ACX Audio Lab, ACX's own free analysis tool, and remember Audio Lab does not detect noise floor or editing errors.

2. Decide: exclusive or wide

  • You have chosen one path before uploading anywhere: Audible exclusive, or wide.
  • If you take ACX exclusive, you accept 50% royalty and you cannot distribute the same title wide at the same time. ACX non-exclusive pays 30%.
  • If you go wide, you skip ACX exclusive and use the direct and distributor routes below. You keep the option of ACX non-exclusive at 30% alongside them.

This is the one decision you cannot undo cheaply. An exclusive deal signed first locks the title out of every other route in this checklist.

3. Route A: Spotify for Authors (direct, free)

  • You have a Spotify for Authors account. Upload is free and non-exclusive. There is no setup fee and no distribution fee.
  • Your audio files are ready: MP3 at 192 kbps or higher, WAV at 44.1 kHz 16-bit, or FLAC, with the same mono or stereo setting on every file.
  • You ticked "This audiobook uses digital voice narration" if the narration is AI. Spotify adds a sentence to the description so listeners know.
  • You know AI-narrated titles stay on Spotify. Spotify does not pass them to its partner stores.
  • You set your price. Spotify pays 50% of the price when someone buys your audiobook, plus a share of a pool when Premium members listen.
  • You allowed about 72 hours for the title to go live after processing.

Free production option, if you have no finished audio yet: Spotify's Audiobook Creation Tools are free for up to 5 titles, open to self-published authors based in the United States, for English-language books. You upload an EPUB file, pick an ElevenLabs voice, preview the audio, and publish. No exclusivity. This is a production tool, not a reason to skip the spec checks above on any audio you produce elsewhere.

4. Route B: Kobo Writing Life (direct, free)

  • You have a Kobo Writing Life account. Publishing is free and non-exclusive.
  • Your files are MP3 only, no file over 200 MB.
  • You asked Kobo to switch on the audiobook tab if it is not on your account yet. It is enabled on request.
  • If the narration is AI, the narrator is listed as Synthesized Voice and the description says so.
  • You set your price knowing the royalty: 45% of list price at $2.99 or more, 35% below that. On a $19.95 audiobook, 45% is $8.98.

5. Route C: Google Play Books (direct, free, gated)

  • You checked whether direct audiobook upload is open to you. Google limits it to select publishers in 12 countries, including the US, UK, Canada, and Australia. If it is not open to your account, skip this route and use Route D instead.
  • If the narration is AI, the narrator is set to Synthesized voice.
  • Your audio is clean, human-quality listening. Google's policy bans low-quality, unclear machine-read audio.
  • You checked your share in the Partner Center before setting a price. Secondary guides report a 52% royalty on direct uploads, but Google does not publish one figure for uploaded audiobooks, so confirm it in your own account. On a $19.95 audiobook, 52% would be $10.37.

6. Route D: Voices by INaudio (wide distribution, no upfront fee)

Use this route for human-narrated audio when you want one upload to reach many stores and libraries at once.

  • Your audio is human-narrated and finished. You arrive with finished files; production is not included.
  • You know the deal before you upload: no setup, upfront, or subscription fees. Voices by INaudio keeps 20% of the royalties it receives and passes 80% to you. It reaches 30+ retail, subscription, and library platforms in 180+ countries, non-exclusively.
  • You worked the example: on a $20 audiobook sold through a store paying 50% of list price, the store pays INaudio $10, INaudio keeps $2, and you receive $8.
  • You know royalties pay monthly, within 30 days of INaudio receiving money from each retailer.
  • You checked INaudio's current policy before uploading an AI-narrated file. Some guides report it does not accept externally produced AI narration, so route AI-narrated titles through the direct portals in sections 3 to 5 instead.
  • You did not list the same title on a store twice, once direct and once through INaudio. Pick one path per store.

7. Route E: ACX, for Audible (human narration only)

  • Your audiobook is human-narrated. ACX requires human narration unless authorized.
  • Your files pass every check in section 1. ACX checks all of them on every file.
  • You chose exclusive (50% royalty, 7-year lock-in on the common terms) or non-exclusive (30%) with your eyes open, and your choice matches the decision in section 2.
  • You know ACX is the only route for an indie author to sell an audiobook on Audible.

8. Metadata, cover, and price (every route)

  • Title, author, and narrator names are spelled the same on every store.
  • The description states the narration type honestly. AI narration is disclosed where the store asks for it, in its own words.
  • Cover art meets each store's size and format rules.
  • Price is set per store, and you wrote down what you keep per sale on each one: Spotify 50% of list price, Kobo 45% at $2.99 or more, Google Play per your Partner Center, INaudio 80% of what the retailer pays, ACX 50% exclusive or 30% non-exclusive.
  • You kept your master files, your cover, and your metadata in one folder, so the next title takes minutes instead of hours.
